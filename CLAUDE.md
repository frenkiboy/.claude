## Reporting Comparisons

When comparing methods/models/conditions, separate **direction** from **magnitude**:

- If A scores higher than B, state it plainly and factually (e.g. "A is higher, 0.93 vs 0.91").
- Reserve **"wins", "improves", "beats", "better"** for a **substantial** effect — one large enough to matter for the conclusion, not just a higher number. An incremental or within-noise edge is **not** an improvement; do not frame it as one.
- Prefer effect-size language over verdict language: report the gap (Δ, fold-change, CI/overlap, whether it survives across seeds/folds) and let the reader judge. A difference that doesn't hold up across seeds/replicates is a tie — say so.
- When unsure whether a gap is substantial, describe it neutrally ("marginally higher", "comparable") rather than declaring a winner.

## Writing clearly (I am a non-native English speaker)

- Lead with the plain-language point; put details after. Give the concrete
  number or object before the interpretation.
- No compressed one-liners or epigrams. If an argument has steps, write the
  steps out literally — do not pack them into a clever sentence.
- Plain English, not idioms or figurative phrasing.
- One new technical term per sentence at most; define it on first use.
- Short sentences. Avoid em-dashes that chain 3+ clauses.
- Expand internal codes on first mention, e.g. "R30-F1b (Report 30, Fig 1b)".
- Reports and summaries: easy-to-read language by default, not academic prose.
- If a number contradicts one I gave earlier, say so explicitly.
